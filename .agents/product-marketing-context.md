# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-03-21*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Framer Bot is a team workspace for operating Framer CMS outside the Framer editor, with AI assistance and role-based access.

**What it does:** Admins connect one Framer project and collection once, then invite teammates into a separate workspace to create, edit, review, and update CMS content. The product adds AI-assisted editing, bulk operations, usage tracking, and team workflow controls so content teams can run Framer CMS without living inside Framer itself.

**Product category:** Framer CMS operations software; external editor for Framer CMS; Framer content ops workspace.

**Product type:** SaaS

**Business model:** Workspace-based SaaS. Current working packaging is Free, Standard ($19), and a later Agency tier ($49+) once multi-workspace and multi-project needs are validated.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Startups, agencies, and content-heavy teams using Framer for their marketing site or blog.

**Decision-makers:** Founders, marketing leads, content leads, growth leads, and Framer freelancers/agencies managing client sites.

**Primary use case:** Let non-designer teammates manage Framer CMS content safely and quickly without needing full Framer access.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Let writers and marketers update Framer CMS without bottlenecking on the one person who knows Framer.
- Add lightweight permissions and team workflows around Framer CMS operations.
- Speed up CMS work with AI in the flow of editing, reviewing, and batch changes.

**Use cases:**
- Startup marketing teams publishing blog and landing-page CMS content
- Agencies handing off ongoing CMS work to clients
- Operators using AI to review, rewrite, and batch-edit CMS entries

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Framer is strong for site building, but ongoing CMS operations can become awkward when multiple non-technical teammates need to contribute regularly.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Giving everyone Framer editor access is expensive or excessive for simple content work.
- Using docs, Slack, or ad hoc review loops adds handoff friction and inconsistency.
- Manual CMS edits inside Framer do not create a clean operating layer for content teams.

**What it costs them:** Slower publishing, founder or designer bottlenecks, extra seat cost, inconsistent metadata, and sloppy handoffs between marketing and design.

**Emotional tension:** Teams feel blocked by a tool they picked for speed, and the person who understands Framer becomes the default bottleneck.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Framer-native CMS workflows and editor seats; small Framer plugins; Notion-to-Framer bridges.

**Secondary:** Webflow Editor-style workflows, Airtable or Notion as the operating layer, generic CMS admin tools, internal dashboards.

**Indirect:** Agencies or founders manually making every content change themselves.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Separate team workspace for Framer CMS operations
- Admin and contributor roles outside Framer
- AI assistance directly tied to CMS records and selections
- Built around one fast setup: connect project, choose collection, invite team

**How we do it differently:** Instead of asking every contributor to work in Framer, Framer Bot turns Framer into the backend and gives the content team its own operating surface.

**Why that's better:** It is easier to learn, easier to delegate, and easier to standardize across a team or client roster.

**Why customers choose us:** They want Framer to stay the publishing engine, but they do not want everyday CMS operations to depend on Framer access, Framer expertise, or manual handoffs.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Why not just use Framer directly?" | Framer is the site layer; Framer Bot is the team ops layer for people who should not live in the builder all day. |
| "We can already use Notion." | Notion helps draft content, but it does not solve permissions, review flow, or Framer-native CMS operations by itself. |
| "This feels niche." | That is the point: it is specifically for teams already committed to Framer and feeling operational pain around CMS work. |

**Anti-persona:** Solo Framer users who are happy editing directly in Framer and have no recurring team workflow problem.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Publishing is blocked by one person, team members avoid Framer, or seat cost feels wasteful for simple CMS tasks.

**Pull:** Faster publishing, lower friction for contributors, cleaner team workflow, and AI-assisted editing.

**Habit:** Teams keep using ad hoc Slack requests, Notion docs, or direct Framer edits because that is what they already know.

**Anxiety:** Fear of breaking the site, introducing sync errors, or adding another tool to the stack.
